# Native direction

FEROMORPH Native treats the computer as a foundry rather than a screen-effects device.

The work is governed by a small set of persistent laws:

1. **Matter seeks contact.** Independent masses tend to remain close enough to form one unstable body.
2. **Rigidity interrupts softness.** Hard-edged elements prevent the sculpture from becoming a collection of smooth blobs.
3. **Ligaments are temporary.** Connections can carry the body for a period and disappear when tension becomes too great.
4. **Stillness is active.** Long reductions in movement make later rupture perceptible.
5. **Every genesis has a material family.** Colour is curated through bronze, iron, porcelain and mineral states rather than arbitrary random RGB values.
6. **Performance is part of the work.** The system must preserve the host computer. Quality may decline before the machine becomes unstable.

The current alpha uses ordinary rasterised meshes. Future versions may replace selected masses with procedural surface meshes, but only after the native performance baseline is proven on the artist's Linux computer.

# FEROMORPH — Artistic Direction

## Premise

FEROMORPH is not an image generator. It is a material condition.

The work presents a body whose anatomy is not predetermined. It grows through negotiation between attraction, weight, internal absence and surface damage. The body may appear intentional, but it possesses no plan and retains no permanent identity.

The central tension is not order versus chaos. It is **formation versus refusal**.

## Behavioural laws

The system should always retain these principles:

1. **The material has agency.** Movement should appear internally motivated rather than decorative.
2. **No state is final.** Stable forms are temporary plateaus, not conclusions.
3. **Change has consequence.** Ruptures leave altered proportions and visible cavities.
4. **Restraint increases attention.** Long periods of minor movement make rare events significant.
5. **Randomness is curated.** Parameters may vary, but the work must remain within FEROMORPH's material world.
6. **Recognition remains incomplete.** The body may suggest organs, ruins, bones or cast matter without depicting them directly.

## Visual language

FEROMORPH should feel closer to sculpture, casting, geology and anatomy than to conventional computer graphics.

Preferred qualities:

- weight;
- asymmetry;
- negative space;
- roughness interrupted by polished edges;
- monumental scale;
- slow camera movement;
- darkness with precise, material-revealing light;
- ambiguous bodily associations.

Avoid:

- rainbow palettes;
- rapid kaleidoscopic motion;
- obvious liquid simulation;
- decorative particle fields;
- symmetrical mandalas;
- recognisable human or animal anatomy;
- interface elements that compete with the work;
- constant spectacle.

## Temporal structure

A complete cycle is deliberately longer than casual viewing.

- **Genesis:** independent masses seek contact.
- **Accretion:** bridges form and the body gains apparent weight.
- **Stillness:** motion approaches zero but never entirely stops.
- **Rupture:** an internal void expands and divides the body.
- **Consolidation:** surviving forms absorb the damage.
- **Metamorphosis:** the system transitions toward a new genetic state.
- **Afterform:** the previous body remains only as structural influence.

The phases are not announced to the public unless the hidden interface is shown.

## Future development

Potential later versions may introduce:

- persistent memory between exhibition days;
- local camera input reduced to anonymous movement fields;
- sound generated from internal stress rather than added as a soundtrack;
- multiple projected bodies influencing one another;
- physical output through captured meshes, CNC milling or bronze casting;
- a long-duration archival mode that records one still image from each generation.
